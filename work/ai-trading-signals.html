03 · AI · Data · Product Experiment

Turning market noise into actionable signals.

An experimental, AI-assisted market monitoring system built to identify abnormal trading activity, summarize signals, and help active traders understand market reactions faster.

The system should not tell you what to think. It should help you notice what deserves attention.
Problem

What wasn’t working

Markets generate far more data than any single person can watch. Active traders either miss meaningful anomalies in the noise, or burn out trying to monitor everything manually.

User Insight

What the user actually needed

The goal wasn't to predict the market — it was to reduce the cognitive load of scanning it. Traders didn't need another opinion; they needed the system to notice what deserved attention and explain why, in plain language.

My Role

What I owned

  • Scoped and built the product as a personal experiment, from problem framing through a working prototype.
  • Designed the core loop: detect anomaly → summarize → explain → let the user decide.
  • Built a volume-anomaly detection layer, an AI explanation panel, and an alert timeline as the first testable slice.
Constraints

What had to be true

  • The system had to stay explanatory, never prescriptive — surfacing signals without telling users what to do with them.
  • Signal quality mattered more than signal quantity: a feed full of low-value alerts would train users to ignore it.
Decision Process
01

Choosing what to detect first

Rather than building broad market coverage, I started with volume-acceleration anomalies — a signal that's cheap to compute, easy to validate, and immediately legible to an active trader.

02

Designing the explanation layer

An anomaly on its own is just a number. The AI explanation panel translates a detected anomaly into a short, plain-language summary of what changed and why it might matter, without predicting a direction.

Solution

What shipped

  • A signal feed surfacing detected anomalies in near real time.
  • Volume-anomaly detection as the first production signal, with room to add sentiment and order-flow signals.
  • An AI explanation panel that summarizes what the system noticed in plain language.
  • An alert timeline for reviewing what fired and when.
Technical Architecture

How it was built

  • A lightweight monitoring layer that ingests market data, computes anomaly scores, and routes flagged events to a summarization step before they reach the feed.
Product Decisions

Trade-offs I made on purpose

  • Deliberately kept the product to a dashboard-style signal feed rather than stock-market clichés (tickers, candlesticks-as-hero), to keep the focus on interpretation over spectacle.
  • Treated this as a validation exercise for a product hypothesis — reduce cognitive load, not just add more data — rather than a trading tool to monetize immediately.

What I Learned

Takeaways

  • Explaining a signal is a harder and more valuable product problem than detecting one.
  • Restraint — showing fewer, higher-confidence signals — is itself a product decision, not a limitation.
